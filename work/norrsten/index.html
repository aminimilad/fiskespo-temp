Norrsten2026

Built to last. A concept identity and website for a Stockholm architecture practice that builds houses and public rooms meant to age well.

Client
Norrsten Arkitekter (concept)
Role
Art Direction, Identity, UX & Web Design
Type
Self-initiated, 2026
Photos
Unsplash (CC0)

(The problem)

Most architecture sites look the same: a white page, a small logo and a grid of renders. The buildings disappear into a portfolio template, and nothing tells you how the studio thinks, or why its rooms feel the way they do.

(The idea)

Build the website the way Norrsten builds houses: few materials, chosen to last. Only black-and-white photography, warm paper instead of white, and one typeface throughout, set huge and tight in plain sentence case, turning negative where it crosses an image, like light falling across stone.

(The solution)

An editorial site in a single grotesk, Inter Tight: large and plainly set for the statements, small and calm for everything you read and click. The front page says the practice in three words. The project index is a list you scan by place and year, and every project opens with its facts (location, year, area, status) before a word of prose. A process page shows how a house begins, from a day on site to a sun-path study and plaster models, and every layout was drawn for the phone too.
